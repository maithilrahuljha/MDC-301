Simple linear regression: fit, inspect, explain

Promise: fit the line that minimizes squared residuals, interpret its coefficients within the observed range, and use a residual plot to notice when the straight-line story is not enough.

Badge: W01-S03 · Line Fitter

The idea in one pass

Simple linear regression uses one input feature to estimate a numeric target. The model is \(Y=\beta_0+\beta_1X+\epsilon\): \(\beta_1\) describes the fitted change in the mean of \(Y\) for a one-unit increase in \(X\), and \(\beta_0\) is the fitted value when \(X=0\). Do not give the intercept a real-world meaning unless zero lies in the observed range. The fit is not chosen by eye: ordinary least squares (OLS) chooses the intercept and slope that minimize the sum of squared residuals. Setting the first derivatives to zero makes residuals sum to zero and makes them orthogonal to \(X\). Solving those equations gives the sample covariance of \(X,Y\) divided by the sample sum of squared deviations of \(X\) for the slope; the intercept follows from the sample means. With \(X=[1,2,3,4,5]\) and \(Y=[2.1,4.0,6.2,7.9,10.1]\), the supplied worked data yield slope \(1.99\), intercept \(0.09\), and a prediction of \(12.03\) at \(x=6\). The line summarizes observed data; it is not a promise outside its range. Check a residual-versus-fitted plot for structure: curvature points to a missed nonlinear pattern, a funnel to changing error variance, clusters to unmodelled groups, and extreme residuals to possible outliers. In scikit-learn, keep \(X\) two-dimensional, fit only on training rows, and inspect coefficients, residuals, and an evaluation metric together.

OLS estimators via first-order conditions

OLS picks the intercept and slope that minimize the sum of squared residuals:

\[ (\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1)=\arg\min_{b_0,b_1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_i-b_0-b_1X_i)^2. \]

Define \(S(b_0,b_1)=\sum_i(Y_i-b_0-b_1X_i)^2\). Take derivatives with respect to \(b_0\) and \(b_1\):

\[\frac{\partial S}{\partial b_0}=-2\sum_i(Y_i-b_0-b_1X_i),\qquad \frac{\partial S}{\partial b_1}=-2\sum_iX_i(Y_i-b_0-b_1X_i).\]

Set both to zero and divide by \(-2\):

\[\sum_i(Y_i-b_0-b_1X_i)=0,\tag{8.1}\]

\[\sum_iX_i(Y_i-b_0-b_1X_i)=0.\tag{8.2}\]

From (8.1):

\[nb_0=\sum_iY_i-b_1\sum_iX_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_0=\bar Y-b_1\bar X.\]

Plug into (8.2):

\[\sum_iX_iY_i-(\bar Y-b_1\bar X)\sum_iX_i-b_1\sum_iX_i^2=0.\]

Using \(\sum_iX_i=n\bar X\) and rearranging:

\[\sum_iX_iY_i-n\bar X\bar Y=b_1\left(\sum_iX_i^2-n\bar X^2\right).\]

The left side is \(\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)(Y_i-\bar Y)\), and the bracket on the right is \(\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2\). So

\[\hat\beta_1=\frac{\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)(Y_i-\bar Y)}{\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2},\qquad \hat\beta_0=\bar Y-\hat\beta_1\bar X.\]

The second-order conditions confirm a minimum: the Hessian of \(S\) is \(\begin{bmatrix}2n&2\sum_iX_i\\2\sum_iX_i&2\sum_iX_i^2\end{bmatrix}\), which is positive definite when \(\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2>0\), i.e., when \(X\) is not constant.

Break it / fix it · an R² misunderstanding

Read this deliberately wrong conclusion. The code can run correctly while the interpretation is still wrong.

r2 = model.score(X, y)
if r2 == 0:
    raise RuntimeError("The regression code failed")
Show the fix and the lesson

\(R^2=0\) means the fitted model is no better than always predicting the training mean on that evaluation set. It does not prove that Python or scikit-learn failed. Inspect the scatter and residual plot, check the split and baseline, and ask whether a straight-line relationship is appropriate. A negative test-set \(R^2\) can also occur when predictions are worse than the test-set mean baseline.
